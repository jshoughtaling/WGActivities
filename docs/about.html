OHDSI Working Group Activities

About the data

This page is a first look at how active each OHDSI working group (WG) has been. It is compiled automatically from public sources, plus a small amount of hand-collected MS Teams counts, and it is refreshed by hand for now (the date on the heatmap page shows the last refresh). It measures visible activity, not the quality or importance of a group’s work: a WG that meets weekly and works in Teams will look quiet here.

What each source counts

Source What is counted Notes
GitHub Commits, pull requests opened and merged, issues opened, issue and PR comments, and releases in repositories assigned to the group. Bot activity is left out. Repository assignments are a draft made from repository names and descriptions.
Forums New topics and replies on forums.ohdsi.org. The forum has no working group categories, so each topic is matched to a group by keywords in its title, then its tags, then its category. About a third of topics are community-wide announcements, and about a fifth match no group.
Community calls Weekly community calls whose title names the group (for example, “Workgroup Spotlight”). About two thirds of calls are community-wide and count for no single group.
Conference Global Symposium working group sessions (2024 to 2026) and tutorials (2024 to 2026), plus Collaborator Showcase entries (2020 to 2026). Regional events are included too: Europe (2020, 2022 to 2026), APAC (2020, 2022 to 2026), India 2024, Africa 2025, Latin America 2026 and OHDSI UK 2026. All are counted in the week the event starts. Sessions are matched by their heading, so they are reliable. Tutorials and showcase entries are matched by title (and authors for showcase entries), so many matches are by topic: a poster about oncology counts for Oncology even if the group was not involved. Tutorials that fit no single group count as community-wide. Regional items that match no group count under “Regional chapters”. Not every regional event publishes a list: for Europe 2022 and 2024 to 2026, India, Latin America and UK there is only the event itself (one linked entry), and APAC 2022 to 2025 lists only items with a linked poster or slides.
Teams Channel posts and replies, and meeting counts, collected by hand for some groups. Counts only. Messages and transcripts are never published. Hatched cells were not collected.

How to read the heatmap

  • Cell color is relative: each source’s weekly counts are ranked against that source’s own range, so a busy GitHub repository does not hide a quiet forum. With “All sources”, a cell is darker when more sources are active in the same week.
  • No activity (empty cell) and not collected (hatched cell) are different. A source can only be quiet in a week it was collected.
  • The last column is the current week and is still in progress.
  • Status compares the last 8 weeks with the weeks before them: new (first activity in the last 8 weeks), active, quiet (activity earlier, none recently) or dormant (none in the range).

Cross-group collaboration

The Collaboration page flags places where groups may want to talk. It uses three signals, all from item titles and links (never Teams content):

  • Named together: a forum topic, pull request, issue, call, showcase entry, tutorial or session whose title names a second group as well as the one it is filed under. “Workgroup Updates” roundup calls are skipped.
  • Shared repository: a repository that is one group’s own and is also listed as shared with another group.
  • Shared topic: a topic (generative AI, FHIR, phenotypes and so on, listed in config/collab_themes.yml) that titles from two or more groups match. “Not yet linked” pairs are groups active on a topic with no item naming both.

Matching is by keyword, so some leads are false alarms (a word like “vocabulary” in an ATLAS pull request), and groups that collaborate without writing it in a title are missed. The topic list is a first draft for the groups to correct.

Known limits

  • Group-to-repository and group-to-forum matching are drafts and have not been reviewed by the working groups. A few repositories dominate some groups’ GitHub counts.
  • Counts are items, not people. One burst of commits counts as many items.
  • Groups that meet and work mainly in MS Teams, email or private repositories are under-counted.
  • A few groups are listed that are not on the ohdsi.org working group page (marked with *).
