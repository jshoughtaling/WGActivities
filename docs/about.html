OHDSI Working Group Activities

About the data

This site has two aims: transparency (what each OHDSI working group (WG) is doing, and where to find it) and collaboration (where groups are working on the same thing and might want to talk). It is compiled automatically from public sources, plus a small amount of hand-collected MS Teams counts, and it is refreshed by hand for now (the Overview page shows the last refresh). It measures visible activity, not the quality or importance of a group’s work: a WG that meets weekly and works in Teams will look quiet here.

What is on each page

Page What it shows
Overview The last complete week at a glance, and a heatmap whose rows can be groups (each expands into its projects), projects or topics.
Groups Every working group, then a page per group: projects, outputs and recent activity, who it works with, and opportunities.
Projects Software and specifications between a group and its repositories: repos, releases, activity, and which projects adapt which.
Topics Subjects that cut across groups, over time and at each Global Symposium, plus shared terms not yet curated.
Connections A network of groups with evidence of shared work, the evidence behind each pair, and similar work in different groups.
Activity A week’s digest, a calendar of upcoming meetings, or outputs over the last year. Download as CSV, copy as a forum post, follow the RSS feed, or subscribe to the calendar (.ics).

What each source counts

Source What is counted Notes
GitHub Commits, pull requests opened and merged, issues opened, issue and PR comments, and releases in repositories assigned to the group. Bot activity is left out. Repository assignments are a draft made from repository names and descriptions.
Forums New topics and replies on forums.ohdsi.org. The forum has no working group categories, so each topic is matched to a group by keywords in its title, then its tags, then its category. About a third of topics are community-wide announcements, and about a fifth match no group.
Community calls Weekly community calls whose title names the group (for example, “Workgroup Spotlight”). About two thirds of calls are community-wide and count for no single group.
Conference Global Symposium working group sessions (2024 to 2026) and tutorials (2024 to 2026), plus Collaborator Showcase entries (2020 to 2026). Regional events are included too: Europe (2020, 2022 to 2026), APAC (2020, 2022 to 2026), India 2024, Africa (2025, 2026), Latin America 2026 and OHDSI UK 2026. All are counted in the week the event starts. Sessions are matched by their heading, so they are reliable. Tutorials and showcase entries are matched by title (and authors for showcase entries), so many matches are by topic: a poster about oncology counts for Oncology even if the group was not involved. Tutorials that fit no single group count as community-wide. Regional items that match no group count under “Regional chapters”. Not every regional event publishes a list: for India, Latin America, UK and Africa 2026 there is only the event itself (one linked entry), Europe 2022 to 2026 also include their weekend workshops and workgroup meetings, read from the agenda PDFs (Europe 2026 has a few typed in where the two-column layout could not be read), and APAC 2022 to 2025 lists only items with a linked poster or slides.
Teams Channel posts and replies, and meeting counts, collected by hand for some groups. Counts only. Messages and transcripts are never published. Hatched cells were not collected.

How to read the heatmap

  • Cell color is relative: each source’s weekly counts are ranked against that source’s own range, so a busy GitHub repository does not hide a quiet forum. With “All sources”, a cell is darker when more sources are active in the same week.
  • No activity (empty cell) and not collected (hatched cell) are different. A source can only be quiet in a week it was collected.
  • The last column is the current week and is still in progress.
  • Status compares the last 8 weeks with the weeks before them: new (first activity in the last 8 weeks), active, quiet (activity earlier, none recently) or dormant (none in the range).

Items, projects, topics and outputs

  • One item, counted once. Collectors file an item under every group it names, which is right for each group’s own counts. Pages that compare across groups (digests, outputs, topics, similar work) merge those copies, so a poster that names three groups is one item there. A pull request that is opened and later merged stays two items.
  • Projects group repositories into the things people know by name (Gaia, Strategus, ATLAS / WebAPI). Multi-repo projects are listed in config/projects.yml, a first draft for maintainers to correct (see Projects and confirmation); every other repository is a project of its own, under the group it is assigned to. A project can record that it extends another (for example, copies of HADES packages that read the GIS extension tables): that is shown as an opportunity, because support upstream would help both. Projects are counted from GitHub only.
  • Topics are the curated keyword lists in config/collab_themes.yml. An item counts for a topic when its title matches; commits and comments are left out, and items tagged to more than four groups do not count toward any one group.
  • Outputs are releases (nightly builds and snapshots left out), symposium posters and talks, working-group sessions and tutorials.
  • Compatibility (which databases, CDM versions and CDM extensions a project supports) is not collected yet.

Projects and confirmation

The multi-repo groupings are proposed from repository names and the group-to-repository map, not by the projects’ maintainers. Each one is marked Draft grouping until a maintainer confirms it, and Confirmed (with the date) afterwards. The Projects page counts how many are confirmed and can filter to the drafts.

To confirm or correct a grouping, use the link on the project’s page (it opens a pre-filled issue), or edit config/projects.yml and add a line such as reviewed: 2026-11-20 by Ann Lee to that project. A project is not marked confirmed by anything else: the site only reads that line.

Data health

Loading…

Each refresh checks every source: whether its newest item is recent enough, and whether its collector ran (collectors are allowed to fail without stopping the refresh, so this is where a failure shows). The ohdsi.org meetings page is edited by hand; the calendar shows a notice when it has not been updated for more than 10 days. The Overview page links here when anything is out of date. The same checks run on every pull request (scripts/validate_config.py and the tests in scripts/).

Calendar

Working-group meetings come from ohdsi.org’s Upcoming Working Group Calls page, which lists about four weeks ahead and each group’s usual schedule; it is read at each refresh. Community calls are on Tuesdays at 11am ET: past ones show their title and recording, later ones are listed as expected until announced. Symposium dates come from the conference list. Join links are not copied: “Join details” opens the ohdsi.org page, which always has the current link. Meeting lengths are not published, so calendar apps show one hour.

Connections and similar work

The Connections page flags places where groups may want to talk. Pairs of groups use three signals, all from item titles and links (never Teams content):

  • Named together: a forum topic, pull request, issue, call, showcase entry, tutorial or session whose title names a second group as well as the one it is filed under. “Workgroup Updates” roundup calls are skipped.
  • Shared repository: a repository that is one group’s own and is also listed as shared with another group.
  • Shared topic: a topic (generative AI, FHIR, phenotypes and so on, listed in config/collab_themes.yml) that titles from two or more groups match. “Not yet linked” pairs are groups active on a topic with no item naming both.

Similar work looks for the same subject in different groups’ items over the last year: named things (FHIR, DuckDB, BigQuery, CDM v5.5) and two-word phrases (“episode table”, “incidence rate”) that appear in issue, pull request, forum, call and symposium titles. A subject is listed when at least two groups each have an item of their own about it, and pairs that no item names together are marked “not yet connected”; those are drawn as dashed orange lines in the network.

Matching is by keyword, so some leads are false alarms (a word like “vocabulary” in an ATLAS pull request), and groups that collaborate without writing it in a title are missed. The topic list is a first draft for the groups to correct.

Known limits

  • Group-to-repository and group-to-forum matching are drafts and have not been reviewed by the working groups. A few repositories dominate some groups’ GitHub counts.
  • Counts are items, not people. One burst of commits counts as many items.
  • Groups that meet and work mainly in MS Teams, email or private repositories are under-counted.
  • A few groups are listed that are not on the ohdsi.org working group page (marked with *).
